## 2. Harnessing the GPT Model to tackle traditional pain-points

This AI-powered advisory strategy integrates the advanced GPT model into the Cyber & Risk practice, allowing the practice to deliver enhanced services to clients. Specifically, this strategy allows for the combination of the GPT model with the firm’s existing historical knowledge bases. Over the years, advisory organizations have accumulated extensive information across their network of firms. The information in these databases include proposals, working papers, deliverables, success stories, , and more, all of which have been meticulously curated and stored. Individual SMEs come and go, but their legacy is stored and preserved in these knowledge bases. Common examples of issues faced by Advisory Services firms include but are not limited to:

* Increased efforts and resources are required to gather and obtain information, credentials, past deliverables and or proposals across the network of firms.
* Time it takes to identify the key stakeholders (colleagues), request the information, translate, and update such information.
* Accessing, identifying, and mapping relevant skills across the firm’s global network of professionals. Although it can be done, it is time and resource consuming, and can become a blocker when rapid responses are required by customers or prospects.

 By integrating the OpenAI GPT model within the advisory organization’s existing knowledge repositories, the firm can:

* Rapidly access and analyze data from previous projects, ensuring that the lessons learned, and best practices are incorporated into new proposals and reports
* Identify trends, patterns, and correlations within the firm’s historical data that may provide valuable insights for addressing current and future challenges
* Enhance the overall quality and relevance of the deliverables by leveraging the collective wisdom and experience gained from the organization's extensive history in the industry
* Identify the key colleagues in the global firm that can contribute to a specific opportunity (the KB of colleagues and BIOs also to be integrated to the AI so deliverables can be linked/correlated to the professionals in the network).

 This strategic integration of the historical knowledge bases with advanced Generative AI technology not only streamlines the processes but also enriches the value the firm delivers to its clients, setting the firm apart from its competitors.

*Key takeaway*: *Unlock the true potential of your firm's intellectual capital by harnessing the power of AI to transform historical knowledge into actionable insights.*

### **3.4 Chargeback model based on the API token consumption**

Using GPT  does not come cheap. The GPT API billing is based on the concept of ‘tokens’, which are the units of text processed by the GPT-API. Tokens are consumed in both the input (request) and output (response) phases of each API call. The number of tokens involved in a request determines the cost of that API call. Furthermore, when using the GPT API for multiple, chained requests, the number of tokens required increases dramatically, as each subsequent request includes the text from the previous requests. This can result in higher token consumption and, consequently, increased costs. It is recommended to keep track of this consumption and can charge-back to each project.

Consider adding to the intermediary system the ability to track token usage and associate the usage with the user's cost center / Project code. With this approach, the firm can centralize the tracking of token consumption and simplify chargeback process. (see section 4.3 "Intermediary Systems for Secure Data Transmission”)

*Key takeaway: Embrace a fair and transparent chargeback model, promoting responsible AI usage and fostering a culture of cost efficiency within the organization.*

## 4. Data Security and Privacy

A critical aspect of the strategy is the comprehensive framework and system architecture that should be implemented to guarantee security (confidentiality). This could be achieved through multiple controls including but not limited to:

* Anonymization, Redaction, and Obfuscation of Data
* Secure Customer Identification
* Intermediary Systems for Secure Data Transmission
* Reliance on third party Assurance to addressing third party risk (supply chain risk)

### 4.1 **Anonymization, Redaction, and Obfuscation of Data**

The implementation of a Generative AI must be done  in a way that it ensures the highest standards of data protection through the following measures:

* Anonymizing sensitive information to prevent unauthorized access though accidental leakage
* Redacting personally identifiable information (PII) and Client Identifiable Data (CID)
* Obfuscating confidential data to further minimize the risk of unauthorized disclosure

Anonymization, redaction, and obfuscation are different techniques that can be employed based on the specific use case and the type of sensitive information involved.

### **4.2 Secure Customer Identification**

To safeguard customer information, the firm should consider integrating the existing CID Scanning factory system to ensure customer identification remains securely within the firm’s  perimeter. This integration:

* Prevents sensitive data from being exposed during GPT API requests
* Mitigates the risk of data breaches and unauthorized access to client information

### **4.3 Intermediary Systems for Secure Data Transmission**

The architectural design should incorporate intermediary systems that manage and transmit only permitted information through GPT API requests. These systems act as a vital component of the overall security architecture, providing an additional layer of protection for sensitive data. These systems:

* Act as a buffer between the firm’s internal knowledge bases and the GPT model, ensuring that only approved information is exchanged between the two.
* Monitor and filter data exchanged between the firm’s systems and the AI model, helping to prevent the accidental exposure of sensitive information.
* Ensure compliance with data privacy regulations and industry best practices, maintaining the trust and confidence of clients and stakeholders.

### **4.4 Reliance on third party Assurance (Addressing Supply chain risk)**

To ensure the security of the OpenAI GPT systems and the protection of the firm's information, it is crucial to rely upon third-party assurance. The dependence on a system owned by a third party raises concerns regarding the security and confidentiality, integrity, and availability (CIA) of the firm's information. As the firm cannot directly verify the security measures implemented by the third party, it is essential to engage an independent body to periodically review and assess the security controls in place. This independent verification provides a greater degree of confidence in the security of the third-party system, mitigating the risks associated with relying on an external provider for critical services.

It is of paramount importance to consider implementing in the contract with OpenAI a security Annex that covers the usual items as per good practice, but that explicitly states the required frequency and ‘scope’ of these individual artefacts that will provide the alternative attestation.

Usually these artefacts are provided by independent audits, certifications, and security frameworks. These assessments typically evaluate the security controls, policies, and procedures implemented by OpenAI to maintain the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of the data processed through their systems.

As an example some third-party assurance mechanism that could be relied upon include but are not limited to:

* Compliance Certifications: OpenAI may hold certifications such as ISO 27001, SOC 2, or other industry-recognized certifications.
* Independent Security Audits: External audits conducted by reputable cybersecurity firms can assess Open Ai’s security posture and confirm the adequacy of its controls. Regular audits ensure that OpenAI maintains a high level of security and continually improves its practices.
* Security Frameworks: OpenAI may adhere to security frameworks such as NIST Cybersecurity Framework, CIS Critical Security Controls, or other well-established guidelines.
* Privacy Regulations: OpenAI should be compliant with data protection regulations such as GDPR or CCPA, ensuring that the necessary measures are in place to protect user data and privacy.
* Continuous Monitoring: It is crucial to emphasize the importance of continuously monitoring Open Ai’s security posture and ensuring that the firm has a well-defined process for addressing any identified security gaps or concerns. This proactive approach allows the firm to maintain a strong security foundation and promptly address any potential risks that may arise.

*Key takeaway*: *Safeguard your clients' sensitive data and maintain their trust by implementing stringent security measures and anonymization techniques to protect against cyber threats.*

## 5. AI Model Quality Assurance and Oversight

This is one of the most complex areas around AI: How do we ensure that the model is running between its parameters, and as per the firm’s expectations, values, and policies. This is a challenge inherent to any AI, and it is a puzzle that has not been yet fully solved.

### **5.1 Continuous Monitoring**

It is crucial to establish continuous monitoring and iterative feedback loops for the AI model, ensuring its performance remains within the desired parameters and aligned with the firm's objectives. This process involves regularly evaluating the model's outputs, refining its training data, and adjusting its configuration to optimize its performance. By doing so, the firm can maintain control over the AI model customization and ensure that its outputs are consistent, accurate, and compliant with the firm's standards and values.

The recommendation of this paper is to having humans in the loop to make sure model outputs are accurate and up to content standards before they’re published. In this case, it is recommended to still utilize the existing QA process in place at the firm, where the deliverable go through extensive technical peer review and final partner QA before reaching the customers.

### **5.1 AI Ethics and Bias Mitigation**

To ensure that the AI-powered consultancy strategy aligns with the firm's values and ethical principles, it is crucial to incorporate AI ethics and bias mitigation strategies. These strategies may include:

* Periodic audits of the AI model to identify and address potential biases
* Training the AI model on diverse and representative data sets to promote fairness and inclusivity
* Implementing fairness metrics and monitoring tools to evaluate the performance of the AI model with respect to different demographic groups
