# Historical Anomaly: The Open Frontier Is Narrowing

*The period of broadly available frontier AI access looks less like a permanent state and more like a brief historical window.*

On February 23, I wrote that the open frontier was a historical anomaly and that access controls would likely ratchet rather than loosen, with national security as the frame.

Sixteen weeks later, on June 12, the U.S. Commerce Department issued an export-control directive cutting off foreign-national access to Anthropic’s two most powerful models, Fable 5 and Mythos 5. Anthropic pulled them for all customers to comply.

I do not take pleasure in the timing. But the pattern is now hard to ignore.

## What has changed

The three forces I outlined earlier are no longer hypothetical. The advice has not changed: Opus-class access is still open, and the teams that build during this window will carry a meaningful head start into the restricted era.

That is the practical implication. The window is real, but it is not permanent.

## Why this matters

The broader lesson is simple: the best tools are not always the ones that remain open forever. Sometimes the most valuable period is the one in which access is still available to build, learn, and ship before the gates tighten.
